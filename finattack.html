Purpose

Extend general cyber knowledge with financial-operational context.

FINATTACK does not replace MITRE ATT&CK or related frameworks. It extends them by representing financial targets, business processes, regulatory context, dynamic risk and economic impact.

The result is a common knowledge layer for threat intelligence, adversarial validation, remediation, risk and compliance.

Taxonomy
Standardises financial-sector threats across actor, operation, target, regulation and impact.
Graph
Correlates actors, campaigns, vulnerabilities, identities, devices, controls and financial processes.
Simulation
Uses the structured knowledge to explore plausible adversarial routes in a controlled digital twin.
Output
Contextualised scenarios, attack paths, risk hypotheses and evidence for decision support.
Financial threat taxonomy

Five layers that translate technical activity into financial risk.

01Threat Actor Layer

Criminal groups, state-sponsored actors, fraud networks, malicious insiders and hybrid organisations.

02Operational Layer

Financially contextualised tactics, techniques and procedures, including credential abuse, transaction manipulation and ransomware operations.

03Financial Target Layer

Digital banking, ACH, SWIFT, Fedwire, RTP, cards, treasury, trading, custody and clearing processes.

04Regulatory Layer

Applicable obligations, control requirements and potential compliance impact by institution and jurisdiction.

05Business Impact Layer

Operational disruption, fraud exposure, financial loss, reputational harm and strategic consequences.

Dynamic risk dimensions

Model behavior that does not fit a static vulnerability record.

Many financial attacks abuse legitimate identities, devices and workflows. FINATTACK incorporates the changing context around each event.

Dimension 01

Identity risk

Privileges, recovery events, account age, linked identities, KYC context and observed trust changes.

Dimension 02

Device risk

Known or unknown device, reputation, fingerprint consistency, emulation and association patterns.

Dimension 03

Behavioral anomaly

Navigation sequence, timing, interaction rhythm, session efficiency and deviation from historical behavior.

Dimension 04

Transaction anomaly

Amount, velocity, beneficiary context, payment rail, device change and time between critical events.

Intelligent financial digital twin

Explore adversarial scenarios without disrupting production.

The digital twin represents systems, identities, devices, relationships, controls and financial processes as a living simulation environment.

AI and graph models can generate hypotheses, explore alternative attack routes and evaluate how control changes affect the probability and cost of reaching a financial target.

1. Build the current state

Represent authorised metadata, architecture, identities, controls and financial processes.

2. Generate scenarios

Use known intelligence, graph structure and controlled models to propose plausible paths.

3. Explore alternatives

Evaluate different initial conditions, privileges, devices, behaviors and transaction sequences.

4. Measure resilience

Compare control effectiveness, path probability, attacker effort and potential business impact.

5. Feed the learning loop

Return simulation results to the knowledge graph with traceability and confidence.
